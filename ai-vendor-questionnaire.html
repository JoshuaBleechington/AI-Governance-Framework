Vendor Overview
Basic vendor identification, AI product description, and deployment context. Used to establish scope of assessment and which subsequent questions apply.
Q1 What is the name of the AI product or feature being assessed, and what business problem does it solve for our organization? INFO
Provide the full product name, version, and a clear description of the specific AI functionality — not just the broader platform. Be specific about which features use AI/ML versus traditional rule-based logic.
Q2 What type of AI/ML techniques does this product use? (e.g. Large Language Model, classification model, computer vision, recommendation engine, etc.) MEDIUM
Understanding the underlying technique helps classify EU AI Act risk tier and identify relevant bias/fairness concerns. LLMs carry different risks than traditional ML classifiers.
Large Language Model (LLM / Generative AI)
Traditional ML (classification, regression, clustering)
Computer Vision / Image Recognition
Natural Language Processing (non-generative)
Recommendation / Ranking Engine
Rule-based / Expert System (not AI/ML)
Q3 Which departments or functions within our organization will interact with or be affected by this AI system? MEDIUM
Identifies scope of impact and which employees, customers, or third parties may be subject to AI-driven decisions. Required for EU AI Act risk tier classification.
Q4 Will this AI system make or significantly influence decisions that materially affect individuals (hiring, credit, healthcare, legal, access to services)? CRITICAL
This is the primary EU AI Act High-Risk trigger. Any affirmative answer activates mandatory conformity assessment requirements, human oversight obligations, and bias testing requirements under EU AI Act Annex III.
Yes — directly makes such decisions
Partially — significantly influences but human makes final call
No — informational/analytical only, no material decisions

Section 02 of 05
Model Transparency
Assesses how openly the vendor communicates about how their AI model works, what data trained it, how decisions are made, and whether outputs are explainable.
Q5 Does the vendor provide a Model Card or equivalent documentation describing the model's intended use, limitations, performance metrics, and known failure modes? HIGH
Model Cards (introduced by Google) are the industry standard for AI transparency documentation. Their absence for any high-risk AI deployment is a significant red flag and may indicate non-compliance with EU AI Act Art. 13 transparency requirements.
Yes — comprehensive Model Card publicly available
Partial — some documentation available under NDA
No — vendor declines to provide model documentation
N/A — rule-based system, no ML model involved
Q6 Can the vendor explain, in plain language, how the AI system arrives at its outputs or recommendations? HIGH
Explainability is required under GDPR Art. 22 (right to explanation), EU AI Act High-Risk requirements, and NIST AI RMF MS-3. Black-box models that cannot be explained are non-compliant for high-stakes decisions.
Yes — full feature importance and decision reasoning available
Partial — general explanation available but not per-decision
No — black-box model, no explainability provided
Q7 What data was the model trained on? Does the vendor disclose training data sources, vintage, and any known gaps or limitations? HIGH
Training data quality directly determines model reliability and bias risk. Vendors using outdated, unrepresentative, or opaque training data introduce significant risk. Required disclosure under EU AI Act Art. 10 (Data Governance).
Yes — full disclosure of training data sources and limitations
Partial — general description provided without specifics
No — training data details are proprietary and undisclosed
Q8 How frequently is the model retrained or updated, and how are customers notified of material changes to model behavior? MEDIUM
Model drift — gradual degradation of performance over time — is a critical governance concern. Undisclosed model updates can alter behavior of deployed systems. Required under EU AI Act post-market monitoring obligations.
Formal change management — customers notified of all material updates
Release notes provided but no proactive customer notification
No disclosure process — model updates applied without notice
Q9 Does the vendor publish accuracy, precision, recall, and confidence metrics for the model in production conditions (not just benchmark tests)? HIGH
Benchmark performance often significantly overstates real-world accuracy. Vendors should provide production performance metrics. Absence of real-world performance data makes it impossible to assess actual risk.
Yes — production performance metrics available and regularly updated
Benchmark metrics only — no production performance data
No performance metrics disclosed
Q10 Does the vendor provide customers with access to audit logs showing what inputs the AI received and what outputs it produced for decisions affecting our organization? HIGH
Audit trail access is required for regulatory investigations, incident response, and demonstrating compliance. EU AI Act Art. 19 requires High-Risk AI systems to maintain logs. GDPR requires accountability evidence.
Yes — full audit logs accessible to customer
Partial — logs available on request but not self-service
No — audit logs not available to customers

Section 03 of 05
Bias & Fairness Testing
Evaluates whether the vendor has proactively tested for discriminatory outcomes across protected characteristics and has controls to detect and remediate bias over time.
Q11 Has the vendor conducted structured bias testing across legally protected characteristics (race, gender, age, disability, national origin) before deployment? CRITICAL
Pre-deployment bias testing is mandatory under EU AI Act for High-Risk systems (Art. 9, 10) and required by EEOC guidance for employment AI in the US. Absence of testing is a critical compliance failure for any decision-making AI.
Yes — comprehensive testing with documented results available
Partial — some protected characteristics tested but not all
No — bias testing has not been conducted
N/A — system does not affect individuals covered by anti-discrimination law
Q12 What fairness metrics does the vendor use? (e.g. Demographic Parity, Equal Opportunity, Calibration, Counterfactual Fairness) HIGH
Different fairness metrics can be in mathematical tension — a model cannot always optimize all simultaneously. Vendors should have a documented rationale for which metric they prioritize and why, based on the use case context.
Multiple metrics with documented rationale for selection
Single metric used — limited fairness perspective
No formal fairness metrics defined or documented
N/A — fairness metrics not applicable to this system
Q13 Does the vendor conduct ongoing bias monitoring in production, not just at initial deployment? HIGH
Bias can emerge or worsen over time as real-world usage patterns diverge from training data. One-time pre-deployment testing is insufficient — continuous monitoring is required under EU AI Act Art. 72 (post-market monitoring).
Yes — continuous bias monitoring with automated alerts
Periodic — scheduled reviews but not continuous monitoring
No — bias testing conducted at deployment only
Q14 If bias or discriminatory outcomes are discovered post-deployment, what is the vendor's documented remediation process and timeline? CRITICAL
Vendors must have a clear, contractually enforceable process for addressing discovered bias. Vague assurances are insufficient — you need specific remediation timelines, escalation paths, and compensation clauses in the contract.
Formal remediation process with defined SLAs and contractual obligations
General process described but no contractual SLAs
No documented bias remediation process exists
Q15 Can customers configure the AI system's fairness constraints or thresholds to align with their specific regulatory context and risk tolerance? MEDIUM
Different jurisdictions and industries have different fairness requirements. Healthcare AI fairness obligations differ from HR AI. Customer-configurable fairness thresholds demonstrate mature AI governance architecture.
Yes — fully configurable fairness parameters per customer
Limited — some parameters configurable within fixed bounds
No — fixed fairness thresholds applied uniformly to all customers
Q16 Has the vendor engaged a third-party auditor to independently validate their bias testing results and fairness claims? HIGH
Self-reported bias testing results have inherent conflicts of interest. Independent third-party audits of AI fairness claims are considered best practice and may be required under EU AI Act conformity assessment for High-Risk systems.
Yes — third-party audit completed with report available
In progress — third-party audit planned or underway
No — self-assessed only, no independent validation

Section 04 of 05
Data Handling & Privacy
Evaluates how the vendor collects, stores, processes, and protects data — including whether customer data is used to train AI models and how data residency requirements are met.
Q17 Does the vendor use customer data (inputs, outputs, interactions) to train, fine-tune, or improve their AI models? CRITICAL
Using customer data to train models without explicit consent violates GDPR and can create confidentiality risks — your proprietary data or customer PII may inadvertently appear in model outputs for other customers (data leakage). Must be addressed in DPA.
No — customer data is never used for model training
Only with explicit opt-in consent and anonymization
Yes — customer data used for training (opt-out available)
Yes — customer data used for training (no opt-out)
Q18 Where is customer data processed and stored? Does the vendor support data residency requirements (e.g. data must remain within the EU or US)? HIGH
GDPR Chapter V restricts transfers of EU personal data to non-adequate third countries. US customers in regulated industries (healthcare, finance, government) may have contractual or statutory data residency obligations.
Customer-configurable data residency with regional processing options
Fixed regions available (EU, US) but not fully flexible
Data processed in single global region — no residency controls
Q19 Does the vendor execute a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) / Data Processing Addendum that covers AI-specific processing activities? CRITICAL
GDPR Art. 28 mandates a DPA for all processors handling personal data. The DPA must explicitly cover AI-specific activities including model training data use, automated decision-making, and sub-processor chains used by the AI platform.
Yes — comprehensive DPA covering all AI processing activities
Standard DPA available but does not specifically address AI activities
No DPA offered or available
Q20 What is the vendor's data retention policy for AI inputs, outputs, and logs? Can customers request deletion of their data (right to erasure)? HIGH
GDPR Art. 17 grants individuals the right to erasure. Vendors must be able to delete customer data from AI systems, including from training datasets where applicable. Indefinite retention of AI interaction logs without justification is non-compliant.
Defined retention limits with self-service customer deletion capability
Deletion possible on request but no self-service — takes >30 days
Indefinite retention — no deletion capability offered
Q21 Has the vendor documented all sub-processors (third parties) that have access to customer data as part of the AI system's operation? HIGH
AI platforms often rely on cloud infrastructure, third-party model APIs (e.g. OpenAI, AWS Bedrock), and annotation services. GDPR requires disclosure of all sub-processors and customer notification of material changes to the sub-processor list.
Full sub-processor list publicly available with change notification
List available on request but not public — changes not proactively notified
Sub-processors not disclosed
Q22 In the event of a data breach involving the AI system, what is the vendor's notification SLA and what evidence of breach investigation will be provided? CRITICAL
GDPR Art. 33 requires breach notification within 72 hours. Vendors must contractually commit to notifying customers within a timeframe that allows the customer to meet their own regulatory notification obligations.
Contractual notification within 24–48 hours with forensic evidence provided
Notification within 72 hours — aligns with GDPR minimum requirement
No contractual breach notification SLA defined

Section 05 of 05
Compliance Certifications
Verifies the vendor holds appropriate third-party certifications, regulatory compliance evidence, and contractual commitments that demonstrate AI governance maturity.
Q23 Does the vendor hold an ISO/IEC 42001 (AI Management System) certification or is certification currently in progress? HIGH
ISO 42001 is the international standard for AI management systems — the AI equivalent of ISO 27001 for information security. Certified vendors have undergone independent audit of their AI governance practices. Absence is not disqualifying but should prompt further scrutiny.
Certified — ISO 42001 certificate available for review
In progress — certification audit planned or underway
No — not pursuing ISO 42001 certification
Q24 Does the vendor hold a current SOC 2 Type II report? Does the report scope include AI-specific systems and controls? HIGH
SOC 2 Type II provides independent assurance over security, availability, and confidentiality controls. Critically, older SOC 2 reports may pre-date the vendor's AI products — verify the scope explicitly includes the AI system being assessed.
Current SOC 2 Type II — scope explicitly includes AI systems
SOC 2 Type II exists but AI systems may be out of scope
No SOC 2 Type II — SOC 2 Type I only or no report available
Q25 For EU AI Act High-Risk systems — has the vendor completed a conformity assessment and registered the system in the EU AI Act database? CRITICAL
EU AI Act Art. 43 requires conformity assessment for High-Risk AI systems before they can be placed on the EU market. EU AI Act Art. 71 requires registration in the EU database. If applicable, failure to comply exposes both vendor and deployer to significant penalties.
Yes — conformity assessment complete and registered in EU AI database
Assessment in progress — expected completion date provided
No — conformity assessment not yet initiated
N/A — system is not EU AI Act High-Risk
Q26 Does the vendor have a published Responsible AI Policy or AI Ethics Framework that has been endorsed by executive leadership? MEDIUM
Executive-endorsed Responsible AI policies signal organizational commitment to AI governance beyond regulatory compliance. Vendors without any ethical AI framework may not have governance infrastructure to handle emerging issues proactively.
Comprehensive Responsible AI policy — executive signed, publicly available
General ethics principles stated but no formal policy document
No Responsible AI policy exists
Q27 Does the vendor contractually commit to notifying customers if their AI system's regulatory compliance status changes materially (e.g. non-compliance finding, regulatory investigation)? HIGH
Deployers of third-party AI share regulatory exposure under EU AI Act Art. 28. If a vendor's system is found non-compliant after you've deployed it, you need contractual protection including notification, remediation timelines, and indemnification rights.
Yes — contractual obligation to notify + indemnification clause included
Notification obligation only — no indemnification
No contractual compliance notification obligations
Q28 Does the vendor provide customers with a documented human oversight mechanism — specifically, can decisions made by the AI system be challenged, reviewed, and overridden by a human? CRITICAL
Human oversight is a foundational requirement under EU AI Act Art. 14 for High-Risk systems and GDPR Art. 22. The mechanism must be technically implemented — not just a contractual commitment. Verify it works in practice, not just on paper.
Yes — full override capability with documented override process
Partial — override possible but requires vendor assistance to implement
No — AI decisions cannot be overridden within the platform
