Tier 1 — Unacceptable Risk
🚫 PROHIBITED

AI systems in this tier pose a clear and unacceptable threat to the safety, livelihoods, and rights of people. These systems are outright banned under Article 5 of the EU AI Act with absolutely no path to compliance — they cannot be deployed in any form.

Legal Status
Completely Banned

None — must be removed

🚫Any vendor proposing such capabilities is immediately disqualified during procurement review
🚫Existing AI tools are screened against prohibited use cases during onboarding
Tier 2 — High Risk
⚠️ REGULATED

High-risk AI systems have significant potential to harm health, safety, or fundamental rights. They are permitted but strictly controlled — requiring conformity assessments, risk management systems, human oversight, and registration in the EU AI database before deployment.

Permitted with Controls

Mandatory Requirements
✅Risk management system documented and maintained throughout lifecycle
✅Training data governance — bias testing, data quality checks mandatory
✅Human oversight mechanism — humans must be able to intervene and override
✅Register system in EU AI database before market deployment
✅Post-market monitoring plan and incident reporting to authorities
Tier 3 — Limited Risk
⚡ DISCLOSE

Limited risk AI systems must comply with transparency obligations. When AI interacts with or generates content for humans, users must be clearly informed they are interacting with an AI system — not a human.

⚡Users must be informed of their right to request human assistance

Function 01 — Foundational
GOVERN
Establish policies, accountability, and culture for AI risk management

GOVERN is the foundation that makes all other functions possible. It establishes the organizational structures, policies, roles, and culture required to manage AI risk systematically — not reactively. Without GOVERN, the other three functions have no structure to operate within.

GV-1
AI Risk Appetite
Define what level of AI risk the organization is willing to accept — and document it formally
GV-2
Roles & Accountability
Assign AI Risk Owner roles with clear responsibilities across security, legal, and operations
GV-3
AI Use Policies
Create acceptable use policies covering AI tool onboarding, vendor review, and disclosure
GV-4
AI Literacy Training
Ensure all staff understand AI capabilities, risks, bias, and responsible use principles
GV-5
Stakeholder Engagement
Actively involve affected communities and business units in AI governance decisions
GV-6
Third-Party Oversight
Establish vendor AI governance requirements and contractual accountability standards
📍 Pursue SEO Marketing — GOVERN Actions Taken
Defined organizational AI risk appetite. Assigned AI Risk Owner role to GRC function. Created AI Acceptable Use Policy and AI Vendor Risk Questionnaire covering all departments.
🗺️
Function 02 — Context Setting
MAP
Identify, categorize, and understand AI systems and their risk context

MAP is about understanding what AI systems exist, who they affect, and what risks they introduce. You can't manage what you can't see — MAP creates the visibility needed for everything that follows. This is where the AI System Inventory lives.

MP-1
AI System Inventory
Catalogue all AI tools in use across every department — including shadow AI
MP-2
Affected Populations
Identify who is impacted by each AI system — employees, customers, third parties
MP-3
Risk Categorization
Map each system to a risk category — align with EU AI Act tiers for dual compliance
MP-4
Data Lineage
Document training data sources, quality controls, and any known bias in datasets
MP-5
Bias & Fairness
Assess potential for discriminatory outcomes before deployment — especially for HR/finance AI
MP-6
Use Case Context
Document the specific business context, decision stakes, and user vulnerability for each system
📍 Pursue SEO Marketing — MAP Actions Taken
Completed full AI System Inventory across 7 departments. Identified 2 vendor tools as EU AI Act High-Risk (HR screening, credit scoring). Documented data inputs and affected populations for each system.
📊
Function 03 — Analysis
MEASURE
Analyze, assess, and quantify AI risks using structured evaluation tools

MEASURE turns identified risks into quantified, tracked metrics. It uses both quantitative tools (accuracy rates, drift detection) and qualitative assessments (explainability reviews, bias audits) to create an evidence base for risk decisions.

MS-1
Performance Evaluation
Track model accuracy, precision, recall — detect drift before it causes harmful outputs
MS-2
Bias Testing
Run structured bias and fairness tests across protected attributes — race, gender, age
MS-3
Explainability Review
Assess whether AI decisions can be explained to affected parties in plain language
MS-4
Vendor Risk Assessment
Apply 28-question AI Vendor Risk Questionnaire to all third-party AI providers
MS-5
Benchmarking
Compare AI system performance against industry standards and regulatory baselines
MS-6
Trustworthiness Dimensions
Score each system across 6 dimensions: Validity, Safety, Explainability, Privacy, Fairness, Security
📍 Pursue SEO Marketing — MEASURE Actions Taken
Developed AI Vendor Risk Questionnaire (28 questions). Created trustworthiness scoring rubric across 6 dimensions. Established model monitoring checkpoints for accuracy drift and adverse outputs.
⚙️
Function 04 — Response
MANAGE
Prioritize, treat, and continuously monitor AI risks across the lifecycle

MANAGE is where governance becomes action. Using the data from MAP and MEASURE, risks are prioritized, treatment plans are implemented, and AI systems are monitored continuously. Learnings feed back into GOVERN — creating a true risk management lifecycle.

MG-1
Risk Treatment Plans
For each identified risk: accept, mitigate, transfer, or avoid — document the rationale
MG-2
Continuous Monitoring
Monitor deployed AI systems on defined cadences — quarterly for low risk, monthly for high risk
MG-3
AI Incident Response
Define what constitutes an AI incident and maintain a response plan with escalation paths
MG-4
Decommission Protocol
Formally retire AI systems that exceed acceptable risk thresholds or fail trustworthiness review
MG-5
CAB Integration
Route AI system changes through Change Advisory Board — same rigor as infrastructure changes
MG-6
Feedback Loop
Feed MANAGE learnings back into GOVERN — update policies based on real-world AI risk data
📍 Pursue SEO Marketing — MANAGE Actions Taken
Tiered AI systems into High/Medium/Low risk bands with review cadences. Integrated AI risk reviews into existing CAB process. Documented AI Incident Response procedure with GRC → Legal escalation path.
